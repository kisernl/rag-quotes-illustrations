### William Carter — The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works

**1.** God has, from the beginning, worked through covenants to reveal Himself, to save a people unto Himself, and to bring His blessings to those whom He has chosen. The covenant established with Abraham, marked by the seal of circumcision, is shown to be a continuation of God’s eternal plan, now fulfilled in Christ and extended to the New Testament Church through baptism. The author takes great care to demonstrate that this covenant, which was once sealed by the blood of circumcision, is now confirmed by the waters of baptism. It is, as he rightly argues, a single covenant of grace under two administrations, the latter being a fuller revelation of God’s mercy and love in Christ Jesus. — William Carter, *The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works*, p. 10 [themes: baptism, church, covenant, eternity, grace, love, mercy]

**2.** In the administration of the covenant of grace under the Old Testament, God used types and shadows, including the type of Canaan. Their passage from Egypt, crossing the Red Sea, and being sustained by water from the rock in the wilderness were all types. Yet, in these types, the Israelites experienced the same communion with God in Christ as believers now experience through New Testament ordinances like baptism and the Lord’s Supper. — William Carter, *The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works*, p. 41 [themes: baptism, covenant, grace]

**3.** The meaning is this: when the Lord bestows mercy and salvation upon humanity, He does so not based on human merit, either actual or foreseen, but according to His own free choice. In His covenant with Abraham, God reveals that His election will not be evenly distributed across the world but will be carried out by families and nations. Thus, God often grants elect children to elect parents and clusters His saints together in certain places and communities rather than distributing them equally across the world. Without this divine arrangement, neither Abraham nor believers could be called blessings in spiritual matters to their families or communities. However, they are blessings precisely because God chooses to bless His elect through them: “He hath blessed us according as he hath chosen us,” (Ephesians 1:3-4). — William Carter, *The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works*, p. 45 [themes: children, covenant, election, family, holy-spirit, mercy, world]

**4.** “The law and the prophets were until John, since that time the kingdom of God is preached.” This does not imply that the Gospel began with John or that the Law ended, but rather that the season of Gospel revelation had come, with the Law’s principles continuing alongside it. — William Carter, *The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works*, p. 84 [themes: law, preaching]

**5.** The Law is kept through Christ’s strength: In the covenant of works, the command was “Do this and live.” Under the covenant of grace, it is “Do this in the strength of Christ and live.” — William Carter, *The Covenant of God with Abraham Opened and Other Works*, p. 87 [themes: covenant, grace, law]

### Thomas Tuke — Love to God

**1.** Affections are the movements of the heart, inclining it toward the object of desire. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 35 [themes: affections]

**2.** If a man’s love is directed toward God, his life will reflect that goodness. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 46 [themes: love]

**3.** Like Jael to Sisera, the world lulls its lovers to sleep with comforts, only to drive a nail through their temples (Judges 4:21). — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 55 [themes: comfort, world]

**4.** The more a man loves God, the more he may be assured of God’s love—not because our love earns His, but because our love is a fruit of His love for us. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 56 [themes: assurance, love]

**5.** It is the lack of love that causes men to forsake God, refuse His service, and leave His house. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 60 [themes: love]

**6.** If someone knowingly continues in sin or values earthly pleasures above God’s commands, their love is counterfeit. While outward actions may appear righteous, if they are done without love for God, they are of no value in His sight. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 72 [themes: love, righteousness, sin]

**7.** Understand this: where Christ is loved, there Christ loves; where Christ loves, there He lives; and where He lives, the flesh and its lusts die. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 78 [themes: death, love]

**8.** If a humble man becomes proud of his humility, he loses the very humility he claims to possess and becomes puffed up with pride. If a man takes pride in his beauty, he forfeits true beauty and becomes a vain fool. If a man is proud of his wisdom, he turns into a fool; if he boasts of his wit, he becomes as senseless as a donkey. If he is proud of his poverty, he becomes rich only in pride; if he is proud of his riches, he becomes poor in piety. What a terrible and hateful sin pride is! Not only is it evil in itself, but it also corrupts good things, turning them into evil. Just as thunder is said to sour good drink, and as venomous creatures transform whatever they consume into poison, so pride turns all the thoughts, words, and deeds of a proud man into instruments of vanity and self-glorification. — Thomas Tuke, *Love to God*, p. 93 [themes: glory, humility, pride, riches, sin, wisdom]

### Richard Resbury — Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism

**1.** What Resbury understood, and what Arminianism so grievously misunderstood, is that election is not a reward but an act of pure mercy. — Richard Resbury, *Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism*, p. 9 [themes: arminianism, election, mercy]

**2.** Those whom He foreknew are the true recipients of the covenant’s eternal and spiritual blessings, and none of them are cast off. The rest, being merely outwardly associated with the covenant, were never truly His people. — Richard Resbury, *Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism*, p. 20 [themes: covenant, eternity, holy-spirit]

**3.** Election is God’s gracious determination to grant life, while reprobation involves His sovereign decision to leave others in their sin. — Richard Resbury, *Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism*, p. 28 [themes: election, sin, sovereignty]

**4.** All things that occur are either done directly by God or permitted by Him as the supreme ruler. Nothing happens apart from His will. — Richard Resbury, *Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism*, p. 33 [themes: providence]

**5.** Election is a decree of mercy, and reprobation is a decree of justice. Yet both are acts of absolute and arbitrary power, favoring or refusing purely at God’s pleasure. — Richard Resbury, *Sovereign Grace Against the Gangrene of Arminianism*, p. 49 [themes: election, justice, mercy, sovereignty]

### Edward Corbet, William Pemble and William Gouge — The Puritans on the Providence of God

**1.** He guides them to his own ends, all the while staying consistent with the nature which he has put in them, such that they voluntarily perform that which he predetermined would certainly come to pass. He causes good actions and permits bad actions; he rules and orders all. — Edward Corbet, William Pemble and William Gouge, *The Puritans on the Providence of God*, p. 41 [themes: providence]

**2.** Wisdom and knowledge are happy instruments of salvation when they are guided by truth. — Edward Corbet, William Pemble and William Gouge, *The Puritans on the Providence of God*, p. 47 [themes: truth, wisdom]

**3.** The natural man, even when that man is endowed with all the excellencies of which the soul is naturally capable, cannot perceive the things of the spirit of God. For he has neither wisdom to make a right choice of the best end, nor understanding to find out the true means. No, he esteems them foolishness, and so changes the greatest blessing into a fearful curse. — Edward Corbet, William Pemble and William Gouge, *The Puritans on the Providence of God*, p. 49 [themes: fear, holy-spirit, wisdom]

### John Durant — Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials

**1.** Afflictions are sent and sanctified to bring us to Christ because often nothing else will. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 39 [themes: affliction, sanctification]

**2.** The improvement of all afflictions should be to bring your soul to Christ. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 52 [themes: affliction]

**3.** Legal humiliation is like a father sending an unruly child to a magistrate, where the child may be punished or even sent to prison. But Gospel humiliation is like a father dealing with the child himself, using a stern tone and hard words to bring the child into submission. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 77 [themes: children]

**4.** First, observe that it is true faith alone that carries the soul through all difficulties and discouragements on the way to Christ. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 151 [themes: faith]

**5.** The whip or a blow may stir the horse into action, but it does not strengthen it. A conviction of need or a fear of Hell may stir us to go to Christ, but it does not enable or enliven us. Beloved, faith is the grace that strengthens. “The just shall live by faith,” (Hebrews 10:38). — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 158 [themes: faith, fear, grace, hell]

**6.** For without faith it is impossible to please God or come to Christ. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 165 [themes: faith]

**7.** When the time comes for you to die, you will then see your need for Christ (if you do not already), and if you do not have a living faith, you cannot have a living Christ, and your soul will be dead before death comes. Faith is much more than the world realizes. We scarcely understand it now, but we will never fully grasp its preciousness until we experience it firsthand. The greatest experience of faith is found in the act of coming to Christ. It is in that process that we truly learn the value, the power, and the price of faith. Whatever else we have or pretend to have, only sincere, pure faith will carry us to Jesus Christ. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 168 [themes: death, faith, world]

**8.** for certainly, just as Jesus Christ will deal kindly with those who truly come to Him at last, so He will deal exceedingly harshly with those who, despite all His kindness, deal poorly with Him. If Jesus Christ (man or woman) will deal well with the soul that waited long, because it waited until the end, then He will deal harshly with you (whoever you are), who, despite all His kindness to you, remain rebellious to this day. O, tremble to think what Christ will do at last! He will judge, condemn, and destroy you, the one upon whom He has waited long and who yet refused to come in. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 185 [themes: judgment, mercy, rebellion, wrath]

**9.** Blessed are we when God’s corrections are our lessons, His punishments our teachers, His chastisements our guidance. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 234 [themes: discipline]

**10.** Among many things, we must consider how to hold our peace, how to be silent without distracting our souls, without complaining against God, and without hindering our learning through affliction. We must be silent in a way that composes our spirits, consents to the Lord’s dealings, and seeks to understand the lessons of affliction. This is our duty, and thus Aaron acted. — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 252 [themes: affliction, peace]

**11.** My brethren, unguarded words are like rebellious enemies; they will break free unless they are contained. You might say, “I am not strong enough to keep a watch.” Then pray, “Lord, keep a watch for me.” — John Durant, *Christ’s Compassion in Our Trials*, p. 265 [themes: prayer]

### Francis Rous — Puritan Meditations

**1.** It is a great disadvantage to mistake good nature for grace, for we deceive ourselves into thinking we have gone far enough toward God because we wish well to all and no harm to any. But it must be known that good nature, even at its best, is not grace. It is full of characteristics that fight against grace, not having in it that true knowledge and love of God nor that inward purity which is required. For when one does what seem to be good works, if they do not proceed from a heart truly sanctified by God, they are nothing more than glossed-over sins issuing from a corrupt fountain and tending to the sea of death. — Francis Rous, *Puritan Meditations*, p. 29 [themes: death, grace, love, sanctification, sin]

**2.** As no man can see God and live, so no natural man can see Christ who is God as long as he lives as a mere natural man. — Francis Rous, *Puritan Meditations*, p. 61 [themes: depravity, regeneration, unbelief]

**3.** The place of faith is first in the understanding, but working downward on the will and affections it joins us to Christ. Faith is not only essential for union but also for all the benefits and fruits coming from that union, of which works are a part. — Francis Rous, *Puritan Meditations*, p. 108 [themes: affections, faith]

**4.** For nothing but God can turn man’s heart to God. … We have also in addition to our fleshly will a will directly contrary to it which hates the former will and the sinful pleasures in which that will chiefly delighted; and loves even the persecutions of Christ, which the natural will especially hated. Thus, by the new birth, light is created, in the midst of the darkness and a rightness of will is formed where there was nothing but crookedness, and such a light and will that they alter the affections, actions, and whole course of man. — Francis Rous, *Puritan Meditations*, p. 134, 139 [themes: affections, love, persecution]

**5.** As the soul is infinitely better than the body, and life eternal than life present, so the food of the soul is far better than that of the body, and the saving of the spiritual life better than the preservation of the temporal life. — Francis Rous, *Puritan Meditations*, p. 168 [themes: eternity, holy-spirit]

### John Durant — Comfort and Counsel for Dejected Souls

**1.** Just as sincerity requires not only serving God but also knowing and willingly doing His will, similarly, in the matter of hypocrisy, it is not merely committing sins but knowing them and hiding them that makes one a hypocrite. — John Durant, *Comfort and Counsel for Dejected Souls*, p. 84 [themes: sin]

**2.** Christ died not only to free you from the condemnation of sin in the future but also to deliver you from its conquest here and now. In His death, you died, and by its virtue, sin within you will be crucified. The Apostle encourages you to reckon on this truth (Romans 6:11). — John Durant, *Comfort and Counsel for Dejected Souls*, p. 95 [themes: death, sin, truth]

**3.** You may misunderstand greatly if you think that dejection is or can be a time for doubt. No, it is the season for faith. — John Durant, *Comfort and Counsel for Dejected Souls*, p. 236 [themes: faith]

### Josiah Shute — Sarah and Hagar

**1.** When God is absent from ruling one’s passions and controlling corrupt affections, only fire remains, and it is a fire that can quickly consume a household. — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 119 [themes: affections, family]

**2.** We should all learn to submit to God’s ways, allowing Him to do with us as He sees fit, even if it means enduring pain for a while. — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 182 [themes: submission, suffering]

**3.** If people could not even look at Moses when God’s splendor shone upon him without him veiling himself, they would certainly be unable to endure the glory and splendor of angels without physical bodies. — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 189 [themes: glory]

**4.** If we do not allow the Law to be our bridle to restrain us from sin, it will become our scourge to punish us for our sins. — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 270 [themes: law, sin]

**5.** Adam’s disobedience in eating the forbidden fruit has distorted the judgment of his descendants to the point where they often speak well of and sympathize with those who hate God and are hated by humanity. — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 284 [themes: judgment, obedience]

**6.** If we do not experience what others have, we have only ourselves to blame. We may want the same favor that David received, but do we repent as he did? We may desire the same favor as Peter, but do we weep as he did? We may seek a return of our prayers as Elijah did, but do we pray as fervently as he did? We may hope for a happy outcome from our afflictions, but are we as humble under God’s hand as His children have been? — Josiah Shute, *Sarah and Hagar*, p. 315 [themes: affliction, children, hope, humility, prayer, repentance]

### Henry Greenwood — The Works of Henry Greenwood

**1.** While there are many ways in the world, only the way of godliness, Christianity, and the Word of God, leads to eternal life. — Henry Greenwood, *The Works of Henry Greenwood*, p. 35 [themes: eternity, scripture, world]

**2.** To believe means to have a firm and resolute assent or consent of the heart to God’s Word, guided by the Holy Spirit toward the salvation of believers. Faith’s material cause is the Word of God, its formal cause is the act of consent, its efficient cause is the Holy Spirit, and its final cause is the salvation of our souls. — Henry Greenwood, *The Works of Henry Greenwood*, p. 60 [themes: faith, holiness, holy-spirit, scripture]

**3.** Christ’s righteousness is like a wedding garment that covers our nakedness on the day of judgment, and faith is the hand of the soul that puts it on. — Henry Greenwood, *The Works of Henry Greenwood*, p. 100 [themes: faith, judgment, righteousness]

**4.** In the world, there is a four-fold invitation, yet only one leads to salvation. The devil says, “Come unto me, but I will destroy you.” The world says, “Follow me, but I will deceive you.” The flesh says, “Follow me, but I will fail you.” Christ alone says, “Come unto me, and I will refresh you.” — Henry Greenwood, *The Works of Henry Greenwood*, p. 260 [themes: satan, world]

### Thomas Hooker — Putting on Christ

**1.** “Just as a wise individual chooses a residence that upholds his reputation and meets all his needs, so Christ finds His dwelling in a humble heart, the most suitable place to manifest His glory.” — Thomas Hooker, *Putting on Christ*, p. 29 [themes: glory, humility, wisdom]

**2.** “We abide in Christ when the eye is fixed upon Christ, when the tongue continually speaks of Christ, when the mind dwells on him, and when the affections are directed towards him. But when the mind is taken off the promise and the comforts found therein, amusing itself by thinking of temptations or inward corruptions, then we are overcome.” — Thomas Hooker, *Putting on Christ*, p. 57 [themes: affections, comfort, promises, temptation]

**3.** “He that thus walks with Christ here, shall live with him forever hereafter.” — Thomas Hooker, *Putting on Christ*, p. 62 [themes: eternity, faith, heaven]

**4.** “But be forewarned, if you will live in your sins here, expect to be damned with them hereafter.” — Thomas Hooker, *Putting on Christ*, p. 83 [themes: sin]

### Nicholas Lockyer — Biblical Sincerity Discovered

**1.** “Sincerity encompasses more than merely praising holiness. It involves actively striving for holiness.” — Nicholas Lockyer, *Biblical Sincerity Discovered*, p. 34 [themes: holiness, praise]

### Josiah Shute — Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague

**1.** “Everyone, whether parents, masters, or rulers, should be obeyed only as far as their instructions align with God’s will. If we choose to ignore God’s commandments to please others, we risk facing judgment both in this world and the next.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 67 [themes: family, judgment, law, obedience, world]

**2.** “For the general populace, recognize the importance of ministers since God has elevated them. He created you without them, but He won’t save you without them. Respect them and follow their teachings, for true respect for a minister lies in practicing what he preaches.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 102 [themes: ministry, preaching]

**3.** “Next, always be wary of persisting in wrongdoing. Pharaoh faced one judgment and then another. Similarly, those who defy God and remain unyielding will experience escalating judgments. Many believe that if they’ve escaped a particular misfortune once, they are safe for a long time. This is a misguided belief.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 109 [themes: judgment]

**4.** “It’s evident that wicked people are more disturbed by the consequences of their actions than by the actions themselves. Pharaoh wants the frogs gone; he doesn’t ask for a change of heart or a fresh start. Sinful individuals are more bothered by the consequences than the sin itself, wishing for physical relief more than the healing of their souls.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 140 [themes: sin]

**5.** “The sin of pride is so ingrained in us that even when other sins decrease, pride grows.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 154 [themes: pride, sin]

**6.** “Beware, if God is not first in His glory, our own glory will be our downfall.” — Josiah Shute, *Divine Judgment and Mercy in the Second Plague*, p. 180 [themes: glory]

### Josiah Shute — Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation

**1.** “One might spend everything to be cured, as did the woman in the Gospel with the issue of blood, yet unless God wills it, it will do no good. Saul might go to the Witch of Endor for help, and Amaziah to the god of Ekron to be healed, but unless the God of heaven works the cure, it will never be done.” — Josiah Shute, *Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation*, p. 30 [themes: heaven]

**2.** “The devil deceives many by presenting sin as attractive, but he lies. Sin is a burden, and hell is as hot as it is described.” — Josiah Shute, *Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation*, p. 46 [themes: hell, satan, sin]

**3.** “True repentance is when a man grieves for his sin to the extent that he abandons it.” — Josiah Shute, *Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation*, p. 50 [themes: repentance, sin]

**4.** “Learn to esteem and value ministers who deal faithfully with your souls. A flattering ministry will ruin the people. Flattery from a minister is worse than flattery from a friend, for such a minister flatters a man not out of his goods and lands but out of his soul and salvation.” — Josiah Shute, *Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation*, p. 106 [themes: faith, ministry]

**5.** “In a regenerated person, God’s Word not only instills a fear of displeasing God but also a determination to strive for complete obedience throughout their life and conduct.” — Josiah Shute, *Ezra’s Covenant Renewal and the Pursuit of a Lasting Reformation*, p. 148 [themes: fear, obedience, regeneration]

### Richard Greenham — quoted in James Nalton, Delay in Reformation Provokes God’s Indignation

**1.** “It is foolish to hide our sores while they can be healed, only to reveal them when they are incurable. Similarly, it is folly to hide our sins while they may be remedied, and later shamefully expose them when they are irreparable. This tangent is brief, as we’ll explore it.” — Richard Greenham, *quoted in James Nalton, Delay in Reformation Provokes God’s Indignation*, p. 89 [themes: sin]

**3.** “Holiness is the best evidence of being in favor with God and of adoption into God’s family.” — Richard Allestree, *The Beauty of holiness*, p. 80 [themes: family, holiness]

### Thomas Case — The Quarrel of the Covenant

**1.** “He that sets God in his presence, dares not sin in his presence.” — Thomas Case, *The Quarrel of the Covenant*, p. 115 [themes: sin]

**2.** “When old sins cost dear, new sins will not find entertainment. When old sins are new afflictions, when the remembrance of them is as wormwood and gall, the soul will not easily be bewitched to drink a new draught of that poisoned cup anymore.” — Thomas Case, *The Quarrel of the Covenant*, p. 102 [themes: affliction, sin]

**3.** “A man’s enemies are these of his own house. No quarrel is so fierce, no feud so mortal, as those between the nearest relations when coals of division are once kindled among them.” — Thomas Case, *The Quarrel of the Covenant*, p. 56 [themes: family]

### Timothy Cruso — Discourses on the Rich Man and Lazarus

**1.** “Whoever prescribes any rules or models to God, besides what he has given and framed himself, deifies his own understanding against infinite wisdom.” — Timothy Cruso, *Discourses on the Rich Man and Lazarus*, p. 118 [themes: wisdom]

**2.** “Idleness is an ideal soil for sins to grow and thrive in.” — Timothy Cruso, *Discourses on the Rich Man and Lazarus*, p. 26 [themes: sin]

**3.** “There is nothing to be endured on earth that can be matched with the pains of hell; all the anguish and horror a soul can feel in this world is more than tolerable when compared with what they feel in that world, where there can be no hope or possibility of relief.” — Timothy Cruso, *Discourses on the Rich Man and Lazarus*, p. 52 [themes: hell, hope, world]
